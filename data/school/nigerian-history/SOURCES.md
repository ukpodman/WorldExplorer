# Nigerian History: sources and verification

Nigerian History was made compulsory from Primary 1 to JSS 3 from the 2025/26 session (Federal Ministry of Education, 3 September 2025). The revised curriculum is introduced cycle by cycle (Primary 1, Primary 4, JSS 1 first).

### How facts are labelled in lessons

* **Documented fact**: recorded evidence (museum records, dated documents, UNESCO inscriptions).
* **Oral tradition**: handed down by word of mouth; presented as tradition, not as fact.
* **Historians’ interpretation**: a conclusion drawn from evidence.
* **Approximate date**: an estimate (radiocarbon dating, style); written with “c.”, “about” or “probably”.
* **Disputed**: historians disagree.

### Facts checked for the first lessons

* Nok: terracottas dated c. 500 BC–AD 200 (The Met); first find 1943 during tin mining near Nok.
* Igbo-Ukwu: first bronzes found by Isaiah Anozie in 1938; excavated by Thurstan Shaw in 1959 at the government’s request; radiocarbon c. AD 850.
* Ife head (British Museum Af1939,34.1): found 1938 at Wunmonije compound, probably 14th–15th century.
* Benin plaque (Af1898,0115.11): leaded brass, 16th–17th century, taken by British forces in 1897.
* Sukur: World Heritage Site 1999 (Africa’s first cultural landscape on the list). Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove: inscribed 2005.
* Kano Chronicle: Arabic, probably written in the 1880s; Palmer’s English translation 1908; early sections treated as tradition.

## Verification status of the topic list

Every topic is marked `scheme`: it appears in a published scheme of work that says it follows the revised NERDC curriculum, but it has **not** been checked against the official NERDC curriculum document, which was not accessible to us. The app shows this label on every topic. Before marking any topic `official`, obtain the NERDC document (from NERDC or a State Ministry/SUBEB) and compare topic by topic.
